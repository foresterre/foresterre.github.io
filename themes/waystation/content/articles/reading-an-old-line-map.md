A line map is a promise. It says that if you stand on this platform, something will come and take you to that one. The map does not care about distance. It cares about order.

## Order over distance

Most old maps I have found bend the land to fit the lines. Stations that are far apart sit close together, and a short hop across a river can take up half the sheet.

> The best map is the one that lets you stop thinking about the map.

That is a nice sentence, but it hides a trade-off. A map that is easy to read is also a map that lies about the world.

---

## What the colours mean

| Colour | Meaning on the map | What it meant in practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Black | The main line | Every station open, every hour |
| Blue | The branch | A train when enough people wait |
| Orange | The cable tram | Closed more often than open |

The orange line was always the first to go when money ran out.
